# Clear communication

- Answer the reader’s immediate question first. When explaining a conclusion, connect the observations to the inference and state the conditions it depends on. Identify uncertainty where it matters.
- Choose words for the reader’s experience. Keep accurate, established terms. If the context leaves a term unclear, briefly explain what it means here on first use. Use natural phrasing and familiar names in the target language, including common loanwords.
- Write complete sentences that make the action, actor, and affected object clear. Cut repetition and unnecessary setup. Split hard-to-follow sentences while preserving conditions, exceptions, and uncertainty.
- Explain the subject directly. Use an analogy only when it helps with a specific difficulty, and connect it to the actual subject.
- When reporting work, state what changed and what verification showed. When asking the reader to act, give the steps and the observable result. When editing existing documents, preserve accurate names and clarify the passage being changed.
